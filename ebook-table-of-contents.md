# Ebook table of contents (revised after full reads)

Target: one book, about 110,000 words. Each chapter lists sources, an action, and a bridge sentence.

All four articles flagged as provisional have now been read in full: *Isn't energy the substrate?*, *Death and continuity*, *Dependent designation and the taste of presence*, and *The logic of dependency*. The structure below reflects them.

## Overlaps found in the reads (resolve before drafting)

1. **Energy appears twice.** The energy section in *The logic of dependency* is verbatim the same as *Isn't energy the substrate?*. Keep the standalone chapter; cut the section from the logic chapter.
2. **Continuity appears twice.** The melody, the candle and the carrier argument are in both *The logic of dependency* (continuity section) and *Death and continuity*. Keep the melody and carrier argument in the continuity chapter; keep the candle and anaesthesia in the death chapter.
3. **The chariot appears four times**: *Dependent designation* (section 1), *Two modes* (section 2), *The logic of dependency*, and *Substrateless World*. Keep the experiential telling in *Dependent designation* as the opening, and cross-reference the rest.
4. **Luminosity** is argued in *Death and continuity* and described in *Dependent designation*. The definitions must match: luminosity is the knowing clarity of the display, not a substance behind it.
5. **Cross-references will break.** *The logic of dependency* refers to Substrateless World chapters by number (Chapters 3, 5, 9, 13, 14, 15) and to "the previous chapters". All of these must be renumbered once the ebook's chapter list is fixed.
6. **Citations to verify before publication**: MMK 1.5 (cited for the salt-and-water example; that example may belong elsewhere); MMK 15.10 and MMK 2.18–20; Visuddhimagga XVI.90 (the path without a goer); Tsongkhapa on accusation and praise as the easiest moment to see the object of negation; MN 72 ("does not apply"); Milindapañha on the lamp.

## Part I: The observer

**1. The witness behind experience**
- Sources: *Dependent designation*, section 1 ("The chariot, before the logic"); *Transition* opening chapters
- Action: keep. This is the experiential opener for the whole book.
- Bridge: the book starts with the sense that someone stands behind every experience. The question is whether that someone can be found.

**2. Vividness without a viewer**
- Sources: *Dependent designation*, sections 3–4 (dependence within appearance; the limits of dependence alone); *Death and continuity*, "Why experience needs no carrier"
- Action: keep. Merge the two luminosity passages.
- Bridge: once the observer is not found, experience does not go blank. It becomes clear that vividness does not need a carrier.

## Part II: The logics and the substrates

**3. Two logics**
- Sources: *The logic of dependency*, sections "Two logics, two starting points", "Do we actually think substantially?", and "What 'logic' means here"
- Action: keep, and move to the start of Part II. It is the method chapter the substrates depend on. Trim the evidence section (space, time, grammar, development, emotion) to its strongest examples.
- Bridge: the substrate the reader has been looking for is not only in things. It is in the way anything is picked out as a thing at all.

**4. The Given**
- Sources: *Substrateless World* §13.4 (manuscript insert 1); *Dependent designation*, section 5 (abstraction and reification; apoha)
- Action: keep. Pair the apoha passage with the Dharmakīrti section.
- Bridge: if there is no observer, perhaps appearance rests on something undesignated. This is the most careful form of that thought.

**5. Conceptual all the way down?**
- Sources: *Substrateless World* §13.8 (manuscript insert 2)
- Action: keep. Pair with chapter 4 as the two replies to the Given.
- Bridge: rejecting the Given invites the opposite error, that everything is concept.

**6. Energy**
- Sources: *Isn't energy the substrate?*
- Action: keep as a standalone chapter. Remove the duplicate from *The logic of dependency*.
- Bridge: the last physical candidate for a carrier is the one physics seems to supply.

**7. Potentiality and plasticity**
- Sources: *The logic of dependency*, section "Objection: Potentiality and Plasticity"
- Action: keep. Cross-reference *Substrateless World* chapter 15, which handles the same move in quantum terms.
- Bridge: if energy is not a store of stuff, perhaps appearance draws on a store of possibilities. The chapter shows why that is a substrate in disguise.

**8. Continuity without a carrier**
- Sources: *The logic of dependency*, section "Continuity Without a Carrier"; *Death and continuity*, "Continuity and the habit" and "What changes in the dying" (the latter kept in chapter 9)
- Action: merge. Keep the melody, the lamp of Nāgasena and the carrier argument here.
- Bridge: a carrier is also imagined to hold a life together across time. The same search, applied to time, finds no carrier.

**9. The candle and the weight of watching**
- Sources: *Death and continuity*, "Continuity and the habit" (end), "What changes in the dying", "The candle, and the weight of watching"
- Action: keep. The death chapter keeps the anaesthesia, Dōgen's firewood and the candle.
- Bridge: if continuity needs no carrier, then death is not the loss of a held thing. The next question is what that changes in practice.

## Part III: Two modes and the middle way

**10. Change without a changer**
- Sources: *The logic of dependency*, section "Change Without a Changer"; Nāgārjuna verse text, chapter 2
- Action: keep.
- Bridge: the grammar of subject and predicate is the last form the substantialist habit takes. Change is met without a thing that changes.

**11. The eight extremes**
- Sources: *The logic of dependency*, section "The Eight Extremes Turned Over" (with the table); Nāgārjuna homage verse; *Substrateless World* chapter 3
- Action: keep. The table moves here as the chapter's centrepiece. Verse commentary goes to the appendix.
- Bridge: the four pairs of extremes are the shape of every question the mind asks about appearance. Each loses its object.

**12. Identity, convention and the criteria of truth**
- Sources: *The logic of dependency*, sections "Identity: From Essence to Criteria" and "Conventional, Not 'Merely' Conventional"
- Action: keep. Link Tsongkhapa's three criteria to *Substrateless World* chapter 13.
- Bridge: once the extremes lose their object, the questions of sameness and validity remain. Both are settled by criteria, not by essence.

**13. Analysis and the dividing mind**
- Sources: *Two modes*, sections 1–4 (dividing cognition; analysis breaks; the middle way; dependent arising and emptiness)
- Action: keep. Analysis is presented as a response to reification, not a standing process.
- Bridge: the extremes are answered by analysis, which shows that what was searched for in an object is not there.

**14. Reification and svabhāva as stance**
- Sources: *Dependent designation*, section 5 (apoha, reification, svabhāva as stance)
- Action: keep. Move here from Part II, since it explains why analysis answers reification.
- Bridge: svabhāva is not a content of experience. It is a stance taken toward appearance, and the same red can be held either way.

**15. Non-dividing seeing**
- Sources: *Two modes*, sections 9–13; *Substrateless World* §10.4 (seeing-as); manuscript insert 3b (reading is seeing)
- Action: keep. The duck-rabbit lives here; §10.4 cross-references it.
- Bridge: analysis answers the habit of dividing. What remains when the habit is not operating is a different kind of seeing.

## Part IV: Beyond cause and effect

**16. The three assumptions and the fourth**
- Sources: *Beyond cause and effect*, sections on the three assumptions and the fourth
- Action: keep. Lead the part with this chapter.
- Bridge: the substrates and the two modes together show what has been dismantled. The fourth assumption, why appearances arise at all, survives them.

**17. Spontaneous presence**
- Sources: *Beyond cause and effect*, sections on spontaneous presence; *Two modes*, section 10
- Action: keep. Remove the duplicated Dzogchen comparison from *Two modes*.
- Bridge: the fourth assumption has no object once seen through. What appearing is, when no producer is sought, is what Dzogchen calls *lhun grub*.

**18. Two traditions, one risk**
- Sources: *Beyond cause and effect*, Dzogchen and Madhyamaka sections; *Two modes*, section 7
- Action: merge. Keep the fuller version here.
- Bridge: the traditions agree on the danger of reifying the ground. Whether they refer to the same nature is contested.

**19. The gap and its absence**
- Sources: *Dependent designation*, sections 6–7 (the gap; analysis as practice)
- Action: keep. Carry the caution about "no gap, no ground" into the text.
- Bridge: if the gap was never holding anything up, the taste of presence is available wherever the construction is not running.

**20. The taste of pacified reification**
- Sources: *Beyond cause and effect*, Bahiya section; *Two modes*, section 12
- Action: keep. Presented as the mark of the habit being seen through, not an attainment.
- Bridge: seen is just seen, heard is just heard. The question is whether this can be kept, and what happens when it is treated as an achievement.

**21. Painted food, painted hunger**
- Sources: manuscript insert 5 (The Painted Rice Cake); manuscript insert 4a (Thought Is Not the Veil)
- Action: keep. Merge the thought-as-display material here.
- Bridge: designation is itself a formation, and the taste is recognised in ordinary acts such as eating.

**22. Recognition, not achievement (conclusion)**
- Sources: *The logic of dependency*, section "A Different Path: Conditions and Openness" and the side-by-side table; *Dependent designation*, section 8 (what the essay can and cannot do); *Beyond cause and effect*, conclusion
- Action: keep, and shorten. Restate the book's line in one page.
- Bridge: the book ends where it began, with the observer not found and nothing needing to be produced to take its place.
