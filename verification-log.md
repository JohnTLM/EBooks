# Primary-text verification log

Final verification pass, 2026-10-22. Each item is either confirmed (with its source), corrected, or removed. Nothing is left as "unverified" in the manuscript text.

## Confirmed

- **MMK 24.18.** Garfield (1995), Sprung (1979), Robinson (1967), Siderits and Katsura (2013) agree on the three-term equivalence.
- **MMK 24.19.** "Because there are no phenomena that are not dependently arisen, there are no phenomena that are not empty." Tsadra glossary (Bodhicitta) citation.
- **MMK 15.2.** Own-nature (svabhāva) defined as non-contingent and not dependent on anything other than itself. JIABS study of MMK 24.18 and 15.2.
- **MMK chapter 2 and the opening verse.** Chapter 2 is Going and Coming (the walker and the walking). The eight negations form the opening verse (the homage). Both are standard features of the MMK's structure.
- **Tsongkhapa's three criteria.** From the *Lhag mthong chen mo* (Great Exposition of Special Insight), as taught by Geshe Jinpa Sonam; also in Thupten Jinpa's 2008 Lam rim teaching. Part III Ch. 12 states them.
- **Longchenpa, groundless ground.** Wikipedia's "Ground (Dzogchen)" article quotes Longchenpa.
- **Ka dag.** Higgins (via KMaps): a term signifying primordial purity as a way of signifying emptiness. Namkhai Norbu: "uncreated, ever pure, not something that has to be constructed."
- **Openness (rig stong, gsal stong).** Higgins (via KMaps); Rangjung Yeshe wiki. Confirms the sources use openness as a characterisation of the basis, which is the book's stated departure.
- **Recognition as awakening.** Wikipedia's "Ground (Dzogchen)" article.
- **Rigpa.** Wikipedia's Rigpa article: knowledge of the ground; opposite ma rigpa; Sanskrit vidyā.
- **Malcolm Smith on rigpa.** Quoted in his DharmaWheel posts (via the awakeningtoreality summary) and in the Wisdom Podcast interview: he translates rigpa as vidyā and holds that "awareness" is wrong.
- **Dharmakīrti's definition of perception.** Dignāga's *kalpanāpoḍha* ("free from conception"); Dharmakīrti adds *abhrānta* ("non-erroneous") (Funayama, JIABS). Nyāyabindu defines perception as free from kalpanā.
- **Bahiya / Udāna 1.10.** Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu's translation on SuttaCentral: "In reference to the seen, there will be only the seen…"
- **Xiangyan.** Burned his writings; awakened when a pebble struck bamboo while sweeping; Guishan's question about the original face (multiple Chan accounts).
- **Gabyō.** Tanahashi and Welch, *Moon in a Dewdrop* (FSG, 1995). The painted-rice-cake passage is confirmed.
- **Genjōkōan (firewood and ash).** Dōgen, Genjōkōan, Shōbōgenzō. The "ash is after, firewood is before" wording is confirmed in Paul Jaffe's translation (1996, in Yasutani, *Flowers Fall*), as shown on the Terebess Zen Literature page for the Genjōkōan.
- **Candrakīrti and self-awareness.** Madhyamakāvatāra ch. 6 critiques svasaṃvedana (MacDonald 2022 lecture). The blade that cannot cut itself is the standard counterexample (Kobayashi, 2022, on Prajñākaragupta's reply to Dharmakīrti's lamp).
- **Candrakīrti on Dignāga.** JIABS study "Candrakīrti on Dignāga on Svalakṣaṇas": Candrakīrti's first chapter engages an unnamed interlocutor whose view closely resembles Dignāga's.
- **Stcherbatsky.** *Buddhist Logic*, vol. II, containing the Nyāyabindu translation. Leningrad: Izdatel'stvo Akademii Nauk SSSR, 1930 (Indo-Iranian Reprints 26). Dover reprint 1962.
- **Noether.** "Invariante Variationsprobleme," *Nachrichten von der Gesellschaft der Wissenschaften zu Göttingen*, 1918, pp. 235–257. English translation: Kosmann-Schwarzbach, *The Noether Theorems* (Springer, 2011), translated by Bertram E. Schwarzbach.
- **Núñez and Sweetser.** *Cognitive Science* 30(3): 401–450 (2006). DOI 10.1207/s15516709cog0000_62.
- **Mipham's Beacon.** John Whitney Pettit, Wisdom, 1999 (Studies in Indian and Tibetan Buddhism). Defends Dzogchen in Madhyamaka terms; discusses Tsongkhapa's eight points.
- **Higgins's thesis.** Investigating the distinction between sems and ye shes (University of Wisconsin-Madison). Reads Longchenpa as rejecting self-reflective awareness.
- **Substrateless World §10.4.** Seeing-as, the duck-rabbit and reading (manuscript insert 3b and the project TOC). Chapter 13 is "Conceptual Designation vs. Mere Naming"; Chapter 14 is "The Role of Function in Conventional Reality"; both confirmed against the Substrateless World text.
- **Dzogchen self-release (rang grol).** Wikipedia's Dzogchen article; *The Natural Freedom of the Nature of Mind* (Longchenpa) confirmed as a text.

Deleted, per the instruction to remove anything that cannot be verified. The grief point, which does not depend on it, is also removed with the paragraph.

- **Part II Ch. 7.** Unverified claim that the quantum-measurement point is developed in a numbered chapter of *Substrateless World* removed from the note.
- **Part II Ch. 9.** Genjōkōan source now given, with the Jaffe translation.
- **Part III Ch. 10, 11 and 13.** Notes now cite the confirmed structure (MMK chapter 2; the eight negations as the opening verse; MMK 24.19 via the Tsadra glossary). The unverified "Ocean of Reasoning" reference in Ch. 12 removed.
- **Part III Ch. 15.** The §10.4 cross-reference and the Nyāyabindu definition are now marked as confirmed.
- **Part IV Ch. 18.** Mipham's "lamp reply" and the Mipham and Gorampa claims about rigpa's luminosity removed. The lamp is re-attributed to Dharmakīrti and Prajñākaragupta; the knife example to the standard counterexample in the svasaṃvedana debate, with Candrakīrti's MA ch. 6 critique as the source.
- **Part IV Ch. 19.** Longchenpa's account of self-liberation attributed to *The Natural Freedom of the Nature of Mind*.
- **Part IV Ch. 20 and 21.** As before (Bahiya as translation; Gabyō paraphrased).
- **Appendix B.** Rigpa: Malcolm Smith's position confirmed. Recognition: the rtogs pa distinction removed (not found in any source). Rang rig: Mipham and Higgins referenced as described in Part IV.
- **Appendix D.** Visuddhimagga entry removed (chapter XVI could not be confirmed). Sprung page 238 removed (not confirmed). Stcherbatsky date confirmed. Noether translation edition confirmed. Mipham credited to Pettit.
